Climate change in Europe is driving biodiversity loss through three primary mechanisms: habitat fragmentation from land-use change, rising average temperatures displacing native species ranges, and increasing frequency of extreme weather events. Northern regions show the fastest warming rates while Mediterranean coastlines face the most severe drought and desertification risk. Key policy responses include the EU Biodiversity Strategy 2030, national habitat restoration laws introduced across 11 member states, and the Emergency Heat Protocols activated in 2025 across six nations.

Which European countries face the highest long-term drought risk?
✦
Based on the 2025 Mediterranean Drought Assessment and three supporting reports, Spain, Portugal, and Italy face the highest risk — with projections worsening significantly after 2035 under current emissions trajectories. Southern France and Greece are classified high-risk from 2040 onwards.

The primary drivers are reduced winter precipitation, earlier snowmelt in Alpine catchments, and increased evapotranspiration from summer heat events.
Mediterranean Assessment 2025 EU Climate Outlook 2024 Alpine Water Report 2025

What policy responses exist specifically for Spain?
✦
Spain's National Adaptation Plan (PNACC 2021–2030) addresses drought through three pillars: water demand management, ecosystem-based adaptation in river basins, and agricultural transition subsidies. A 2024 speech by the Environment Ministry flagged implementation gaps in the Ebro and Tagus basins specifically.
